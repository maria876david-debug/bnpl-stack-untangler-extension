# Black Friday With BNPL: Take the Deals Without Stacking the Due Dates

Black Friday is the one week of the year when buy now, pay later stops feeling like a budget tool and starts feeling like a slot machine. Every checkout offers pay in 4, every discount has a countdown, and each individual plan looks harmless. The damage isn't in any single plan. It's in the pile.

## How the pile actually forms

Pay in 4 takes the first instalment at checkout, then one every two weeks. Order on Black Friday and your payments land roughly on the day itself, mid-December, the end of December and mid-January. Now do that at five stores. You've built yourself ten December payments out of five "interest-free" decisions, and they arrive in the most expensive month of the year.

The trap is that no single screen shows you the pile. Each app shows its own plan, its own dates, its own tidy progress bar. Nobody shows you the combined week where four plans all take money within 48 hours of each other — and of rent.

## Three rules that keep the deals and drop the damage

**One combined cap, not five separate ones.** Decide the total you'll put on BNPL this week — a single number — before you open the first app. "I can afford $40 per plan" is how five plans become $200 a fortnight. "I can afford $150 total, across everything" is a budget.

**Buy in date order, not discount order.** If two carts are competing, check which checkout puts fewer payments into your heaviest week. A slightly smaller discount with dates that land after payday beats a bigger discount that lands three days before it.

**Write the dates down where you'll see them.** Every plan, every date, one list, before the parcels arrive. The five minutes this takes is the entire difference between using BNPL and being used by it — late fees and declined-payment stress don't come from the buying, they come from the forgetting.

## The deals are fine. The calendar is the problem.

Nothing here says skip Black Friday. If the price is real and the item was already on your list, pay in 4 spreads the cost exactly as advertised. Just treat the due dates as part of the price, because your bank account will.
