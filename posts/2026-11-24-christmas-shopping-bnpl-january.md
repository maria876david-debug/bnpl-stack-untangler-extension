# Christmas Shopping With BNPL: Keep December Plans Out of January

December is buy-now-pay-later season. Every shop offers to split the cost, and every split feels small — until January arrives with four or five plans, all due in the same fortnight. The trick is not to avoid BNPL in December. The trick is to keep December's plans from owning January.

## The December trap, in one sentence

One plan at a time is manageable; five plans started in the same week all come due in the same week, and the total is never what you pictured when you clicked "pay in 4".

## Three rules before you split anything

**One list, one total.** Before you buy, write every plan you already have open — provider, amount, next due date — and add the new one to the list. If you can't see the total, you can't afford the next plan. A plan you forgot about is still a payment you owe.

**Stagger the due dates.** If two plans fall due on the same day, ask whether you can move one — some providers let you shift a payment date by a few days. Spreading due dates across the month matters more than the size of any single instalment.

**Count the real cost of a missed payment.** Most BNPL plans charge no interest if you pay on time, but a missed payment can mean a late fee, a frozen account, or a mark on your credit file depending on the provider. The cheapest plan is the one you never miss — so only start plans whose payments land on days you know money will be there.

## The January test

Before you confirm a December plan, ask one question: if my January pay is a week late, can I still cover every instalment due that month? If the answer is no, the plan is too big, or there are too many of them. A smaller gift list beats a January of missed payments and late fees.

## After Christmas, close the loop

When the last instalment of a plan clears, check that the provider marked it paid and closed. Keep the confirmation. Plans that stay "open" on a provider's system after you've paid them are a headache you don't need in the new year.
