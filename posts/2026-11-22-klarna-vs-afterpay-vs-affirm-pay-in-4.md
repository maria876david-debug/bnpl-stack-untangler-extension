# Klarna vs Afterpay vs Affirm: Pay in 4, Side by Side

Three names dominate buy now, pay later in the US, and at checkout they look interchangeable: split the price into four payments, usually every two weeks, with the first taken today. Underneath that shared shape, Klarna, Afterpay and Affirm differ in the places that matter once something goes wrong — a missed payment, a return, or a bigger purchase that does not fit the four-payment mould.

## Where they are the same

All three Pay in 4 plans are built for smaller baskets, run over roughly six weeks, and are marketed as interest-free when you pay on time. All three approve you at checkout in seconds, and all three will restrict your account if payments are missed. If you only ever buy one thing at a time and always pay on the due date, the three feel identical — which is exactly why the differences catch people out.

## Where they differ

**Affirm** is the one built for bigger tickets: alongside Pay in 4 it offers longer monthly plans, and those longer plans can charge interest — the rate is shown before you confirm, so read that screen rather than assuming every Affirm plan is free. **Klarna** also stretches beyond Pay in 4 with longer financing on larger purchases, again with terms that change by plan. **Afterpay** stays closest to the original model — four payments, smaller limits that grow with good behaviour — and is the most explicit about late fees when a payment is missed. Late-fee policies, limits and what gets reported differ by provider and by plan, and they change; the plan screen at checkout is the contract, not the advertisement.

One more habit separates people who use these apps comfortably from people who get caught out: check each app once a week, not on the due date. Limits, due dates and plan statuses change inside the apps — a returned item can alter a payment, a rescheduled instalment can move a date — and the person who looks weekly sees it in time. Five minutes across three apps is cheaper than one late fee.

## The real risk is stacking, not choosing

Whichever name you pick, the danger is the same: plans do not talk to each other. A Klarna plan from one store, an Afterpay plan from another and an Affirm plan for something bigger can all land payments in the same week, and each app only shows its own slice. The provider matters less than the full picture — every plan you hold, every due date, in one list you actually check before payday.
