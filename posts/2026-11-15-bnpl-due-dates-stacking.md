# How Stacking BNPL Due Dates Goes Wrong — Quietly, Then All at Once

No single buy now, pay later plan feels dangerous. Each one is small, split into friendly little payments. The trouble starts when the plans stack — and every app is designed so you never see the stack.

**Each app shows you only itself.** Klarna shows you Klarna. Afterpay shows you Afterpay. Affirm shows you Affirm. Nobody shows you the total. So you judge each new purchase against one plan's balance, not against everything already scheduled to leave your account.

**The due dates interleave.** Most plans bill every two weeks, but they start on different days. In the sample stack on BNPL Stack Untangler — four plans across three apps, $265 still owed over 12 payments — the calendar shows weeks where three payments land within days of each other: $35 on Tuesday, $25 on Wednesday, $15 on Friday. Each one looked harmless at checkout.

**The real risk is the pre-payday pile-up.** In that same sample, $95 comes due in a single week, all of it before payday, and more than a typical pay period's spare room. That is how stacking actually goes wrong: not one huge debt, but several small ones quietly agreeing to arrive in the same week your account is at its lowest.

**What fixes it is one combined view.** A single calendar with every plan lined up against your paydays turns surprises into decisions. You can see the tight week coming, keep that amount aside from the paycheck before it, and know your actual debt-free date — in the sample, December 11, if everything is paid on schedule.
